# Design

What users see, as the project's owner decided it. Only their decisions are here: what an agent picked and they haven't corrected is not, and is free to change. Before changing how a screen looks or behaves, read its section; a rewrite carries every line of it over. A decision that replaces one here deletes it in the same change. Nothing here describes a screen: the code does that.

## Everywhere

- Follow Jakob's Law. It is the first rule and comes before every other one here: people spend most of their time in other apps and on other operating systems (ours has an OS feel to it), so ours works the way those already do.
- A word on a screen is the one iOS, Android, Google or GitHub uses for the same thing.
- What a device does itself still follows the device: scrolling, the keyboard, the back gesture, its pickers and its share sheet.
- Each platform's conventions are followed (17 Nov): an iPhone's text size, its bounce, a list passing under the bar and the home indicator, a status-bar tap to the top; a row's ⋯ menu also opens on a long press or a right-click; a swipe on a row only for a clear Delete or common action already in that menu; no pull to refresh, since lists update live. Cmd-, stays unbound for now.
- Three layers. Principles, the same everywhere: content first; order from type size and weight, not boxes, outlines or shadows; colour is for app icons and for warn or danger ("this needs you"), never for "tap this": a button's weight says how much it matters; fine is silent (no "Connected" or green); a problem shows on the thing itself, as one problem box (symbol, title, one sentence, the action that fixes it); an empty screen is one grey line, with a button only for an obvious first step; one component per job.
- Our look, the same on every client: shadcn's design principles, language and component names, with no platform tint. Its values follow Raycast's model (19 Nov: "Yes! This is the direction and I love it."): neutral, one text colour at five strengths, windows and panels of glass with Raycast's shadow and inner edge, and its row, bar and footer as measured beside it, in Allo where Raycast has the system font. One `default` button per view, then `secondary`, `outline` and `ghost`; red only for `destructive`. Our apps' icons are Raycast's style (19 Nov), replacing flat tiles of one solid colour: a macOS squircle, a gradient of several stops, a glyph of two or three tones with its own shadow, a highlight along the top and an inner edge. A third party's app (Gmail, Google's apps, Chrome, WhatsApp, Messenger, iMessage, Apple's apps) and a brand anywhere, in widgets too, keep that company's official icon or logo, unmodified: never redrawn, tinted or put on a tile, and never a generic glyph. Red count badges, Phosphor icons. Every screen has Raycast's anatomy through the UI library's components (19 Nov: its bar, footer pills and ⌘K action panel, rows, metadata, forms, sheets, alerts, toasts); an app moves to its own Raycast-inspired layout (the Vault's list beside its item, its Copy Token) when it next changes for another reason. Layout by container queries: each part sizes itself, grids fill the room, lists stop at a readable width, and what a list opens shows beside it where there's room. Never a page width. Screens are the UI library's components, through `ui`, by choice, not by rule: a screen is written in Solid. Every screen in this repo is on it.
- Nothing zooms in the app: no pinch, and never into a field that's tapped. A field's text is never under 16px on a touch screen, which the UI library's fields and base styles see to; a browser's page keeps its own zoom (18 Nov).
- The typeface is Allo at roundness 100, with the device's own monospace for code, the same on every client; a script it lacks falls back to the device's own.
- Text is one of a few type styles (`core/ui/src/tokens.ts`): screens and apps use them, never a weight or size of their own. Weights sit between Allo's named ones, a little lighter in dark; big titles and figures are tracked in; numbers that change or line up are tabular. Weight doesn't animate.
- The platform's own: only what the UI library can't do (passkeys, keychain, notifications, the scanner, files, home-screen widgets, the Mac's menu bar item). Sheets, menus and alerts are the UI library's, the same everywhere.
- A failure's trace id is for developers: the CLI and MCP show it, never a person's screen.
- Text is short: one idea per sentence, the fewest words that still say it, nothing the reader doesn't need now. That's screens, notifications, CLI output, MCP tool descriptions and skills.
- The real thing, not counts: a small set is listed ("on You and Work"), never "added to 2 pages". No version numbers or rows of figures added to look like a reference. The one exception is an app's version, in the App Store only (17 Nov).
- One way to do each thing. No second version of a screen to save a step; fewer screens, modes and variants beat a shortcut.
- Something that takes a while shows what it's doing where its result will be, one state at a time: its steps while it starts (`Steps`), a spinner and a line while it's under way (`Status`), on what's there now when it's busy with that, and a problem box with Try Again once it's failed. A hold-up of a few seconds isn't a problem: only one that lasts is. (Andre, 16 Nov.)
- Screens are for people; the CLI and MCP are for agents.
- A device says whether it's on in its grey line, never with a dot or the word "Offline": "Online" while its socket is open, else "Seen 2 minutes ago" (screens, long form) or "seen 2 min ago" (CLI and MCP), and nothing for one never seen. This device says "This device" instead. Everywhere a user picks a device (Devices rows, Run Priority cards, Enabled Services columns, an app's Runs On, where schedules run) each one says it; a call to a device that's off says "mac was last seen 2 min ago", never "offline". Mockups skipped on purpose (Andre, 11-11): this is the best fit for the rules above, so change it freely later.
- A screen works fully within what MCP Apps allows (no reliance on notifications, a navigation stack, sheets outside its frame or native features), so it's the same in our clients and in someone else's.
- Motion: the browser's own transitions and animations inside a screen, and its View Transitions between an app's screens. Between the launcher and an app, and from app to app, the window moves the two live frames itself (Web Animations, driven by its move machine), so a move can be turned back midway, a tap during it isn't lost, and a finger can drive it (18 Nov). When the user asked for reduced motion, it's Apple's way (17 Nov): what would slide, grow or zoom is a short cross-fade instead, never switched off, and a page dragged by the finger finishes with a fade rather than jumping.
- Names people read are specific and title-cased (17 Nov): "Apple Calendar" next to "Google Calendar", never just "Calendar"; "Encryption", never an id like `encryption`. A long name is cut with "…"; there are no separate short names. The one exception is "the One App", whose "the" is lowercase everywhere, even at the start of a sentence or a title (so "the PTC App", not "The PTC App"; 18 Nov).
- People see apps, never "plugin" (17 Nov). An app with several launcher icons (app entrypoints, in code) is still just an app to them.
- Version numbers show only in the App Store (17 Nov).
- Reuse a screen before building one (17 Nov): an app with no screen of its own opens its App Store page.
- When something can't be done, it says why where the person tries it (17 Nov): Remove App greyed, with "This is Family's, and you're not one of its owners." Never a missing button with no reason.
- Removing anything asks first, in an alert that names what goes with it (17 Nov), as iOS's Remove App does.
- Admin things (infrastructure apps, managing users) show only to the people who can act on them, the network's owners (17 Nov). Everyone else never sees them.
- A section header's See All sits on the title's baseline, smaller than the title, ending at the content's edge (17 Nov), as iOS's App Store does.

## Screens (18 Nov)

- Every app's screens and the launcher are written on the UI library's primitives, so they run natively on our renderer on a computer, Android and an iPhone, and unchanged on the DOM on the web and in MCP Apps.
- The contract (18 Nov) is built from low-level primitives the way GPUI is: a box, a scroll (a box that scrolls), text, an image, a path (a vector shape) and a text input, with events (click, hover, focus, keys, scroll), focus, and transitions and animations. Hosts (the DOM and our renderer) draw only these. An input's state and events are Flutter's editing state: its text, selection and composing range, and its action (Done, Go, Next, Search, Send).
- Design tokens are typed values in one TypeScript module of the UI library, each varying by condition (dark, phone, touch, reduced motion), never a stylesheet written by hand. Styles are static, typed objects beside the component that uses them, of tokens only, resolved by the build ahead of time: the last style applied wins, with no cascade, and a component says by its types which properties a caller may override. A raw colour or length in a static style is a warning, "make it a token", and a raw font fails the check; an app's own tokens (`defineVars`, `createTheme`, as StyleX's) and dynamic styles (a function of its values, as StyleX's) take any valid value.
- One build makes every target's: the web's CSS (variables and atomic classes), and for our renderer token and style tables, which travel with the UI library and each app over the air, versioned. A host knows kinds of token and the conditions, never a token's name or value.
- Every UI library component is written once, on those primitives, and is the same code on every host; its look stays exactly the one described here.
- Everything written this way updates over the air.

## App windows (18 Nov)

- On a computer, every app opens as its own window. Every app, no mix of the two ("that's just confusing"). One process, the One App's, draws every app's window (19 Nov), as Zed's and Raycast's windows are drawn, so another open app costs a few MB, not a process of its own; each app still runs in its own sandbox. So the One App has one Dock or taskbar icon and one Cmd-Tab or Alt-Tab entry, with every app's window under it (its Window menu, the Dock icon's menu, Mission Control), and on a Mac the menu bar is the front window's app's (its name, Edit, Window). As Raycast's (19 Nov), that icon and entry are there only while an app's window is open: the launcher alone has neither, the One App getting them as an app's window opens and losing them as the last one closes, and the launcher's panel never has a taskbar button. The Home Screen is the launcher (Launcher), and opening an app from it opens or brings forward that app's window. Phones and the web are unchanged.
- Each app is one of the computer's apps too, kept in step with what's installed: on a Mac in `~/Applications/the One App/`, which Spotlight and Launchpad find, its icon the app's own on macOS's icon grid; in Windows' Start menu; and in a Linux desktop's applications. Opening it opens that app's window, or brings it forward.
- An app's window has nothing of its own but the app: "Updated · Reload", "<App> didn't open", Update Required and the Reconnecting banner show over it as they do over an app on a phone.
- Closing an app's window ends it. Quitting the One App (its menu bar ring's menu, ⌘Q, Ctrl-Q on Linux) closes every app's window.
- Every app's window has a way Home (18 Nov). On a Mac, no title band: "Nice quality apps get rid of that but still keep the traffic lights." The app runs to the window's top, its title kept for Mission Control, the Window menu and Cmd-Tab; its title bar is the page's top bar, a unified toolbar as macOS's System Settings and Raycast have (19 Nov): 52 points, the traffic lights in its middle where macOS puts them in one, the breadcrumb after them, and the screen's Back and Forward and title in the same row, beside what's open in a split; held where nothing in it is pressed it drags the window, and double-clicked it zooms as the Mac is set. Just right of the traffic lights, a breadcrumb: the One App's ring and "Home", lighter, highlighted under the pointer, tooltip "Home Screen"; a muted "/"; then the app's own icon and name in semibold, which isn't a button. Home, and Window → Home Screen (⇧⌘H, as Finder's and Safari's Home), show the launcher and leave the app's window open. Windows and Linux the same (18 Nov): on Windows the app runs to the top, the breadcrumb top left where the title was, and Windows' own minimize, maximize and close stay on the right, drawn by Windows, Snap Layouts on hover, as Edge, Teams and File Explorer have them; on Linux a GNOME header bar, as Files and Settings have, the breadcrumb at its start and the desktop's own window buttons on whichever side the person set.

## Launcher (18 Nov)

- On a computer (Mac, Windows, Linux) the Home Screen isn't a window but a launcher, as Spotlight and Raycast are: its only job is starting apps, each of which opens in its own window (App windows).
- It's summoned by its shortcut, ⌥Space on a Mac, which macOS leaves free, and Super+Alt+Space on Windows and Linux, where Alt+Space is the window menu's and Super+Space the input source's; by the One App's icon in the Dock or taskbar, while an app's window gives it one; on a Mac by Open in the menu of the ring in the menu bar, which a click opens, as Raycast's does (its menu has Open, Settings… and Quit); and by Home in any app's window. The shortcut can be changed (no settings screen yet: a `shortcut` file in the client's folder).
- A panel with no frame, its search and its card as one centred on the screen the pointer is on, across and down, in what the menu bar and the Dock or taskbar leave clear (19 Nov), or where it was dragged to on that screen, as Raycast's is (19 Nov: held anywhere in its search or card that isn't pressed or typed in, it moves), in which two surfaces of glass float separately (Andre's pick, mockup 1 "Pair", 19 Nov): the search on top, and under it the card. Each is the system's glass with Raycast's shadow and edge: Liquid Glass on a Mac (vibrancy before macOS 26), over the desktop; Windows' Acrylic and Linux's popover colour likewise. On a Mac it shows over full-screen apps too.
- It goes once an app opens from it, on Escape, or once something else is clicked. the One App keeps running with it put away, holding the connection and notifications, until it's quit.
- The card holds Home's pages, exactly the phone's, the same layout and widgets, with the owner switcher at its top and the page dots at its foot, as on the phone (19 Nov). The card is one size, always, as Raycast's is (19 Nov): three pages side by side as seamless columns, each an iPhone Home page's grid, four columns by six rows, with widgets in their usual 2×2, 4×2 and 4×4 cells, turning one page at a time; an owner with fewer pages has them in its middle. The pages slide as a phone's Home Screen's do (19 Nov): a trackpad's two-finger swipe moves them one to one, giving at the first and last page, and let go, a spring that keeps their speed settles them on the nearest page, or flicked, the next; the arrow keys and the dots slide them by the same spring, and the dots light along as they move. For whoever asked for less motion, a quick cross-fade instead. The App Library cross-fades in within the same card, which keeps its size and scrolls what's taller, and an opened category grows from its square; Escape goes back. Editing, dragging and widgets are as everywhere else.
- The search is the One App's own, keeping Raycast's look (19 Nov): a large, bare "Search apps…", with no box, magnifier or ring, which has the keyboard as the launcher opens and keeps it. No footer pills and no Actions ⌘K.
- Motion is the point (19 Nov): real springs, interruptible and keeping their speed; Home, the App Library and what's found cross-fade in the card, which never changes size.
- A plain highlight, as Raycast's, under the app or widget chosen: the first app on the pages as they show (as Raycast chooses its first item), then the arrow keys move it; a chosen widget brightens. ← → go along the pages shown and turn the page at either end, on to the App Library past the last; ↑ ↓ go up and down a page, and past its top or bottom to the owner above or below. The pointer moves it too. Return opens what's chosen. The glyph goes to the App Library, and Escape back. Tab moves the keyboard through what can be pressed, as anywhere (19 Nov). The search has the keyboard each time the launcher shows, and its shortcut's key, held or pressed again as it shows, types and presses nothing.
- Typing turns the card into what's found, in the One App's own style (19 Nov), at the card's size, scrolling what's taller: under Apps, the widget of the first app found, where it has one, then the apps found as icons; under Widgets, other widgets that match. Only what exists is grouped: apps and widgets. Names that start with what's typed come first, then those with a word that does, then the rest, each A to Z; the first is chosen for Return, and the arrow keys choose. Escape empties the search, then puts the launcher away. Shown again within 90 seconds, it's where it was, what was typed too; after that it forgets it and comes back to Home's first page, as Raycast's Pop to Root Search (19 Nov).
- Signing in, Update Required, "Updated · Reload" and the Reconnecting banner show in the launcher, over its Home Screen.

## The rail (18 Nov)

- One way on phones and tablets from any app back to the Home Screen, which adds nothing to the app, as iOS and Android stay out of an app's way. Its model is iOS's home indicator.
- Phones and tablets: a home line (18 Nov), iOS's home indicator turned on its side, about 4 × 64 pt, centred top to bottom on the right edge, dark over what's light and light over what's dark, as iOS's is. It's over apps only, never the Home Screen, and out of the way while something is open over the app (a sheet, a menu, the window's own dialogs). Pulling it in makes the app follow the finger and shrink to a card over the Home Screen, as iOS's swipe home does; past a short distance, or on a quick flick, it goes home, into its icon if it came from one, from any of the app's screens; short of that, it springs back. A tap does nothing. For reduced motion, the Home Screen fades in. Its touch area is wider than the line. On Android only the line's own stretch of the right edge is kept from the system's back; on an iPhone, WebKit's swipe between pages is off, so its right edge is the line's, and the window's own swipe in from the left edge goes back: home at an app's first screen, one screen deeper in.
- The web is the same.
- The computer's own app (Mac, Windows, Linux) has no rail and no recent apps (18 Nov): "That was a YAGNI at this stage." Each app is a window of its own, whose breadcrumb goes Home (App windows), and the Dock or taskbar and Cmd-Tab or Alt-Tab go between apps, as they do between any of the computer's apps.
- An app's first screen has no Back to the Home Screen: the rail, a computer's breadcrumb, and the device's own gestures (Android's back, the trackpad's swipe, the iPhone's swipe in from the left edge, Escape), leave it. An app's own Back stays for its own screens.
- On a computer, where the breadcrumb names the app, its first screen has no big title of its own (18 Nov): its bar keeps only its buttons. Deeper screens keep their title and Back; no deeper crumbs (YAGNI). Phones keep their titles, as iOS's large titles.

## Home Screen

- Only Home is per owner (17 Nov): each owner (You first, then Everyone, then your groups) has several Home pages side by side. After them comes one App Library, for everyone you can reach. Home and the App Library are its two sections, each at least a whole screen (18 Nov): a swipe moves one screenful, through Home's and on past an owner's last Home page to the App Library, as on iOS.
- The App Store is an ordinary app (18 Nov): its icon is in the App Library, under Utilities, and can go on a Home page, and it opens and closes as every app does. It's no section of the Home Screen.
- A page has no title at its top, and keeps the gap a phone's own home screen has.
- Pages never scroll: a page is a fixed grid, and what doesn't fit goes on a new page. Widgets never scroll either: a fixed number of rows, and tapping one opens its app.
- Left and right move between an owner's pages. Up and down on Home moves between owners, sliding vertically; in the App Library it scrolls it. Each owner stays on the page it was left on.
- There's no tab bar and no Dock (18 Nov), following iOS.
- The owner switcher is a small pill at the top of Home in the middle (17 Nov), in Liquid Glass: "You ⌄". It's on Home only (18 Nov). Tapping it opens a list of every owner; picking one slides there. Swiping up and down still moves between owners, and the pill's name rolls with them. Long names are cut short.
- The page dots are their own small Liquid Glass pill, fixed at the bottom in the middle, just clear of the home indicator (18 Nov): one dot per Home page, one even with a single page, then a small App Library glyph (a 2×2 grid) at its end. The glyph says there's one more page, and tapping it goes to the App Library; on the App Library it lights. It doesn't move as the owners change (17 Nov): the next owner's dots roll in with their name, and it takes their number. Sliding along the dots moves between pages too.
- The App Library's search is in the same top row as the owner pill, its style and height (17 Nov).
- The App Library holds every app you can reach, once, whether it's installed for you, a group or everyone (17 Nov), by category as an iPhone's App Library: four small icons, one opening its app, anywhere else opening the category. It's one full-width page that scrolls up and down, as iOS's (17 Nov): its search at the top, then its categories in a grid in the categories' order, two across on a phone and more on a wide screen; no pages and no dots of its own: it's the glyph at the end of Home's dots (18 Nov). Every installed app is there (17 Nov). Background apps, those with no entrypoint of its own, are under Background Apps, below the rest (17 Nov): a row flush with the background, muted text and a chevron, closed until it's tapped, as iOS's collapsible sections are (Files' sidebar, Mail's mailboxes); open, it has the same category folders, holding only background apps. Each device remembers it open or closed. Tapping a background app opens the App Store at its page, where it's managed; its hold menu has no Add to Home Screen, and it can't go on a Home page. Search finds apps in both, and a match among background apps shows under Background Apps, open. An app's hold menu says who it's installed for. What only the network's owners manage (Database, Encryption, Sandbox, Permissions) is in Admin, which only they see. An opened category has no owner above it, and grows from its place and back into it, as an iOS folder does.
- New apps land in the App Library, not on a page: pages hold only what's put there. An app about you (Settings, Notifications, AI Usage and the device apps) is in your App Library even when it's installed for everyone, and no app is in two.
- Desktop and tablet: nothing beside the pages (18 Nov). As many whole pages side by side as fit, every one of them on an ultrawide. A screenful with fewer pages has them in its middle. Each is exactly the phone's page, nothing on it moved, drawn larger to suit the window's height, at most twice, so a 4K screen shows another page rather than bigger icons (17 Nov); in a computer's launcher, smaller, to fit its panel (Launcher). Up and down moves between owners. The same elements, centred: the owner pill at the top in the middle, the dots pill at the bottom in the middle. Nothing there the phone doesn't have. Those controls (the owner pill, the dots pill, + and Done, the search field) never grow with the pages or the window: under a pointer they're a computer's own size, as compact as macOS's toolbars and search fields, and under a finger a fingertip tall, on a phone and a tablet alike (18 Nov).
- Editing Home pages follows iOS's own, in its gestures, states and look. Touch and hold an app or empty space, then Edit Home Screen (right-click on a computer). Icons jiggle, each with ⊖, which takes it off the Home Screen and leaves it in the App Library. Drag to reorder, and hold at a page's edge to move to the next page, a new one past the last. + opens the widget gallery: pick a widget and its size, then place it. Tapping the page dots opens the page overview, to reorder, hide or delete Home pages. + is at the top left and Done at the top right, either side of the owner pill in its row (17 Nov). Done, or a tap on empty space, leaves. From the App Library, touch and hold an app, then drag it onto any owner's Home pages (17 Nov): Home slides in on the owner you were last on, holding at a side edge turns the page and at the top or bottom edge the owner, for an app already on a page too. Dropped where it's installed, it's placed; where it isn't but you may install it, a sheet asks "Install <App> for <Owner>?", and Install installs it there, its progress on its icon where it was dropped, or puts it back in the App Library saying why it couldn't; where you may not, a sheet says you can't add apps for them and who can, and it goes back. Moving an icon never uninstalls anything, and Done keeps every owner's pages as edited. A computer has the same edit mode, as Launchpad does.
- Uninstalling is in the App Library (17 Nov): touch and hold an app, then Remove App, which asks in an alert naming what goes with it ("Remove Google Account? Gmail needs it and will be removed too.") and puts it in Recently Deleted. Where you may not, Remove App is greyed, with why under it: "This is Family's, and you're not one of its owners."
- Whoever may arrange an owner's pages edits them; anyone else gets only the app's own menu on touch and hold, with no Edit Home Screen. An owner's pages are one layout: editing everyone's or a group's changes them for everyone who sees them.
- Home Screen behaviour copies iOS's unless there's a reason not to (17 Nov): paging, long press into edit mode, the hold menu, drag with the other icons reflowing, haptics, and an alert before removing.
- An app opened from its icon grows out of it and closes back into it, as iOS's (17 Nov), and one opened from its widget out of the widget (18 Nov); one opened any other way slides in. For whoever asked for reduced motion, either fades in place. On a computer, Escape leaves an app at its first screen, with nothing open in it, as the device's back does (18 Nov).
- The page dots are all one size, the current one brighter, as iOS's (17 Nov).
- Tapping a dot goes to its screenful, and the App Library's glyph to the App Library (18 Nov).
- Editing moves no icon (17 Nov): + and Done are in the owner pill's row, and the dots pill stays, so nothing jumps. Tapping a dot opens the page overview, and the App Library's glyph stays, dimmed and not tappable (18 Nov).
- Big app icons on pages have their name under them (17 Nov), cut with "…" when long. The cards stay square: the icon shrinks to make room. Why: our icons aren't as well known as iOS's, so the name is needed.
- Nothing is hidden (17 Nov): the App Library always shows everything installed, and Remove from Home Screen only takes an app off a page.
- Widgets come in a phone's own sizes: 2×2, 4×2 and 4×4. A widget's title is low in its order.

## Settings (18 Nov)

- Settings is one app, in Utilities, where every app brings its settings, as Raycast's Settings has its extensions'. Its sidebar: the search, whoever's looking with Sign In & Security under their name, the sections in three groups, then every app they have, A to Z, with an Update pill where one's ready.
- What an app adds to another section's page (a device's) is a group headed by that app's icon and name, so it's clear which app brings what.
- On a phone the sidebar is cards, and each section is pushed as a page, as iOS's Settings.
- Its anatomy follows Raycast's Settings, not only its values (18 Nov): a sidebar of rows with their icons; a section's page as grouped cards of 46px rows, each heading over its grey line, with headings above the cards and destructive actions as red rows; Back and Forward in one pill beside the title; no footer pills or Actions ⌘K, as Raycast's Settings window has none.

## Vault

- The one place for everything a person has given access to is Settings' Accounts (18 Nov), one page: what's asked for as banners at the top, the accounts, Passwords and Keys, then one section for each other kind of item an app keeps there (Chrome Profiles), under its app's icon and name. A password's or key's line names each app that uses it, by its icon and name.
- Accounts are every app's, in one list, each with its app's logo; Add Account under them.
- An item is ready, busy or needs_action, as its app says: ready shows nothing; busy shows a spinner and what's under way; needs_action shows a dot, why and the one action that fixes it (Sign In, Try Again). Signing out is that, or deleting the item.
- Deleting an item is soft delete's, as for anything an app made: it's in Recently Deleted for 30 days.
- An item opens over the page; one an app keeps is drawn by that app itself (a Chrome profile by Chrome Profiles).

## App Store (17 Nov)

- Browsing and getting apps, quiet: managing what's installed is the App Library's (17 Nov), so there's no Installed.
- Marketplace: one row for each of the nine app categories, cut so the next app shows, with See All; apps the size they are on the Home Screen.
- An app's page has Get (17 Nov), which asks who to install it for, or installs it at once where there's only one owner you may install for; then a row for each owner, with Open and its state there, its version where it's installed; then Requires, each requirement's title and why, never "What you get", and Required by (17 Nov); then See All.
- Its own pages work inside it as an app's do (18 Nov): the Marketplace, a category, an app's page and its See All, with Back in the top row past its first screen, which has none, as no app's first screen has.
- An app's page is laid out as iOS's App Store's (18 Nov): the top row under the status bar, with Back, and the app's name small in it once the big one has scrolled away; then a large icon with the name, what it is and Get (or Open, once there's nobody left to get it for) beside it; then the owners' rows and what it is.
- Trusted and untrusted are said plainly. An update available, a failed install with why and Try Again, and for what was uninstalled, Uninstalled with a button to Recently Deleted, the one place things come back from.
- Install installs what it requires too, saying why. Uninstalling one that others require warns first that they go too.
- Uninstalling puts an app and its data in Recently Deleted, as one item, whose Restore brings both back for 30 days.
- An owner you can't install for shows a plain line, "Only Andre can install for Everyone", with no button.

## Devices, Sign In & Security and AI

- Passkeys are managed inside the app.
- AI is one short prompt pointing the AI at the network's llms.txt.

## Opening, the connection and updates (17 Nov)

- The window's states and the connection's are the owner's signed-off machines (`client.js`, `State` in core/client): a new state or move is theirs to sign off.
