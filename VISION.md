# Vision

I want:

1. Any AI assistant of mine, from anywhere, to act as me across all my devices and accounts, with nothing walled off. It never has to ask for access. It's intelligent, so it judges for itself when to check with me, as a colleague would before sending something or doing what can't be undone. A conversational confirmation is enough.
2. Nothing to belong to one AI provider. If I switch, or use several at once, nothing is lost.
3. Whatever software we need, by asking for it. My AI makes it, and any of us can have it changed when it doesn't fit. Each device has one app, installed once, and everything we make appears inside it, with nothing more to install. It's for everyone in my family or organisation and the groups inside it, and for our AIs. What we make can also sit where I already look on a device, like a phone's home screen or a computer's menu bar, not only in a window I have to open, with real freedom over what it shows there.
4. Where something has no other way in, my AI to use a screen the way I would: a browser or a computer today, and a phone once that's possible. It can work on a screen of its own, kept on a device we already have or in the cloud when there isn't one, or on mine when I want its help there.
5. Anyone to be able to run their own for one to four coffees a month, whatever their size. That's the cost when our own devices do the work. Choosing the cloud instead costs more, is our own choice to pay for, and is still kept low. One general version is in the app stores, built to stay within their rules, and works for any group. A group that wants its own name and look can make its own.
6. It to feel familiar and fast. Where people already know how something works, it works that way here. It's as fast as it can be within that cost.

So:

- One place reaches everything I have.
- Whatever is made here gets what software ordinarily needs without building it: it keeps data, uses my accounts, notifies me, and works while I'm away. It opens only the data of whoever it's for: me, one group, or everyone.
- It's for personal and internal tools, not for serving the public. That's what keeps it small.
- Our own devices do the work when they're on: we already have them, and some things only exist there. A small piece running on a device reaches those. When no device is on, the rest still works, on something we pay for only while it's used.
- That place is worth stealing, so a break-in should get as little as possible. My things open only on my own devices, while one of my AIs is asking, or for software doing its own work. What stores or carries them can't read them, and whatever helps sees only what it's asked to do.
