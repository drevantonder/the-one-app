# Vision

I want:

1. Any AI assistant of mine, from anywhere, to act as me across all my devices and accounts, with nothing walled off. It never has to ask for access. It's intelligent, so it judges for itself when to check with me, as a colleague would before sending something or doing what can't be undone. A conversational confirmation is enough.
2. It to be fast. Opening something, or my AI doing something, should feel instant, the way the best software on that device does. Speed should feel like quality: fast and well-tuned, never the slowness people put up with in workplace software. It opens, scrolls, types and goes back as well as the best apps on a mid-priced phone, and a CLI command or an MCP call is held to the same standard. Speed decides how it's built, not something added later.
3. Nothing to belong to one AI provider. If I switch, or use several at once, nothing is lost.
4. Whatever software we need, by asking for it. My AI makes it, and any of us can have it changed when it doesn't fit. Each device has one app, installed once, and everything we make appears inside it, with nothing more to install. It's for everyone in my family or organisation and the groups inside it, and for our AIs. What we make can also sit where I already look on a device, in whatever places that kind of device has for it, like a phone's home screen, a Mac's menu bar or Windows' widgets, not only in a window I have to open, with real freedom over what it shows there.
5. Where something has no other way in, my AI to use a screen the way I would: a browser or a computer today, and a phone once that's possible. It can work on a screen of its own, kept on a device we already have or in the cloud when there isn't one, or on mine when I want its help there.
6. Anyone to be able to run their own for about one coffee a month for a family of roughly four, which is about $5, and an organisation for less per person than that. That isn't rigid: $10 a month is also fine. The more people use it, the less it costs each of them. That's the cost when our own devices do the work. Choosing the cloud instead costs more, is our own choice to pay for, and is still kept low. Setting it up is simple, and never so much that it would overwhelm someone. A group that wants its own name and look can make its own. A group can also take its own copy and change how it works, and keeping that copy up to date is easy. Nobody should need to, unless they're changing One Core: everything else goes into apps.
7. It to feel familiar. Where people already know how something works, it works that way here. People sign in the way their family or organisation already does.
8. Not to lose things. A mistake can be undone where people would ordinarily expect to undo it, and losing every device, or the place it's all stored, loses nothing.
9. What's made here to get better. It reports how it's doing, so the AI that made it, or whoever looks after the whole thing, can improve it. Everything made keeps its history, and AIs have what they need to build, test and fix it.

So:

- One place reaches everything I have.
- Whatever is made here gets what software ordinarily needs without building it: it keeps data, uses my accounts, notifies me, and works while I'm away. It opens only the data of whoever it's for: me, one group, or everyone.
- It's for personal and internal tools, not for serving the public. That's what keeps it small.
- One Core stays small. Anything heavier, like a phone emulator for someone building apps, is added the same way as anything else made here.
- Whatever is kept here belongs to someone: a person, a group, or everyone. Whoever it belongs to can share it with one person, a group or everyone, to use or to change, and it stays theirs. Only what belongs to everyone is looked after by whoever looks after the whole thing.
- What's made is open to everyone inside once it's in use: anyone can see how it's made and how it's doing, and take their own copy. What someone is still working on can stay private. What people put in it is not open. What's mine and not shared is mine alone, and a group's is its members'.
- All of it belongs to our family or organisation. Whoever looks after the whole thing can get into anything when it's needed, such as for support, moving it or an audit, and only on purpose, except what someone has encrypted for themselves. Users have the privacy they need, and the owner isn't responsible for keeping their credentials safe.
- Our own devices do the work when they're on: we already have them, and some things only exist there. Software of ours running on a device reaches those. When no device is on, whatever doesn't need one still works in the cloud, on something we pay for only while it's used, including work on a schedule and a screen for my AI. What lives only on a device, like reminders on a Mac, waits until it's back.
- Devices on the same network reach each other directly, not by way of the internet. A server still needs the internet to reach the bucket. A server without a connection to the bucket isn't in a normal state. Nothing is designed around it.
- That place is worth stealing, so a break-in should get as little as possible. My things open only on devices looking after me (my own, my group's or everyone's), while one of my AIs is asking, or for software doing its own work. What I've encrypted opens only on devices I chose. We trust whoever stores them, as people ordinarily trust a cloud provider. What I want kept safe from everyone, like passwords, is encrypted, and nothing that stores or carries it can read it. Whatever helps sees only what it's asked to do.
- Data loss is prevented by storage in the cloud, not necessarily compute in the cloud.
- It runs on Mac, Linux, Windows, iPhone, Android and the web.
- It's built for networks of up to about 20,000 users, all in one network with many servers: more than half its users bring their own, about one group in eight has one, and the network runs about one for every 50 to 100 users.

Everything above this line is the thing. None of it is left out.

Would be nice:

- One general version in the app stores, built to stay within their rules, that works for any group. Until then, each family or organisation makes its own.
- Devices in different places reach each other directly, without going through the cloud.
- A record I can read of what my AIs did as me.

Building it:

- AIs do the building, for the people steering them. They can try a change on every kind of device we support, see it running, and check their own work before calling it done.
- At first, those building it run the latest development version for real, so the newest changes reach real devices quickly and get dogfooded. Later, those building it can run the newest version while everyone else stays on a steadier one.
- While it runs, it shows what it's doing: what's slow, what's failing, what it costs, and anything that makes it worse to use, for a person or for an AI. No one has to dig for it.
- The codebase follows best practices in an era where agents do the development: the right way is the easy way because the code's structure makes it so, work is proven on the real thing, and it is tidied constantly.
- Many AIs and their people can work on it at once, on several machines, without getting in each other's way.
- What slows a builder down gets written down and fixed.
- Store releases are kept few, so iterating stays fast and cheap.
