# Vision

I want:

1. Any AI assistant of mine, from anywhere, to act as me across all my devices and accounts, with nothing walled off. It never has to ask for access. It's intelligent, so it judges for itself when to check with me, as a colleague would before sending something or doing what can't be undone. A conversational confirmation is enough.
2. It to be fast. Opening something, or my AI doing something, should feel instant, the way the best software on that device does. Speed decides how it's built, not something added later.
3. Nothing to belong to one AI provider. If I switch, or use several at once, nothing is lost.
4. Whatever software we need, by asking for it. My AI makes it, and any of us can have it changed when it doesn't fit. Each device has one app, installed once, and everything we make appears inside it, with nothing more to install. It's for everyone in my family or organisation and the groups inside it, and for our AIs. What we make can also sit where I already look on a device, in whatever places that kind of device has for it, like a phone's home screen, a Mac's menu bar or Windows' widgets, not only in a window I have to open, with real freedom over what it shows there.
5. Where something has no other way in, my AI to use a screen the way I would: a browser or a computer today, and a phone once that's possible. It can work on a screen of its own, kept on a device we already have or in the cloud when there isn't one, or on mine when I want its help there.
6. Anyone to be able to run their own for one to four coffees a month, whatever their size. That's the cost when our own devices do the work. Choosing the cloud instead costs more, is our own choice to pay for, and is still kept low. Setting it up is simple, and never so much that it would overwhelm someone. One general version is in the app stores, built to stay within their rules, and works for any group. A group that wants its own name and look can make its own. A group can also take its own copy and change how it works, and keeping that copy up to date is easy.
7. It to feel familiar. Where people already know how something works, it works that way here. People sign in the way their family or organisation already does.
8. Not to lose things. A mistake can be undone, and losing every device, or the place it's all stored, loses nothing.
9. What's made here to get better. It reports how it's doing, so the AI that made it, or whoever looks after the whole thing, can improve it. Everything made keeps its history, and AIs have what they need to build, test and fix it.

So:

- One place reaches everything I have.
- Whatever is made here gets what software ordinarily needs without building it: it keeps data, uses my accounts, notifies me, and works while I'm away. It opens only the data of whoever it's for: me, one group, or everyone.
- It's for personal and internal tools, not for serving the public. That's what keeps it small.
- The core stays small. Anything heavier, like a phone emulator for someone building apps, is added the same way as anything else made here.
- Whatever is kept here belongs to someone: a person, a group, or everyone. Whoever it belongs to can share it with one person, a group or everyone, to use or to change, and it stays theirs. Only what belongs to everyone is looked after by whoever looks after the whole thing. It has a history, so any change can be undone.
- What's made is open to everyone inside unless whoever it belongs to keeps it private: anyone can see how it's made and how it's doing, and take their own copy. What people put in it is not open. What's mine and not shared is mine alone, and a group's is its members'.
- All of it belongs to our family or organisation. Whoever looks after the whole thing can get into anything when it's needed, such as for support, moving it or an audit, and only on purpose.
- Our own devices do the work when they're on: we already have them, and some things only exist there. A small piece running on a device reaches those. When no device is on, whatever doesn't need one still works in the cloud, on something we pay for only while it's used, including work on a schedule and a screen for my AI. What lives only on a device, like reminders on a Mac, waits until it's back.
- Devices on the same network reach each other directly, and keep working with the internet down.
- That place is worth stealing, so a break-in should get as little as possible. My things open only on my own devices, while one of my AIs is asking, or for software doing its own work. What stores or carries them can't read them, and whatever helps sees only what it's asked to do.

Everything above this line is the thing. None of it is left out.

Would be nice:

- Devices in different places reach each other directly, without going through the cloud.
- A record I can read of what my AIs did as me.

Building it:

- AIs do the building. They can try a change on every kind of device we support, see it running, and check their own work before calling it done.
- I run the latest development version in my own network, so the newest changes reach my devices quickly and I can dogfood it.
- While it runs, it shows what it's doing: what's slow, what's failing, what it costs, and anything that makes it worse to use, for a person or for an AI. No one has to dig for it.
- The codebase follows best practices in an era where agents do the development.
- Many AIs can work on it at once, on several machines, without getting in each other's way.
- What slows a builder down gets written down and fixed.
